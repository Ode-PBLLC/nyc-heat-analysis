Data + Resources

I. New York City map

Sources: NYC Department of City Planning, Borough Boundaries (26b, water areas excluded) and 2020 Neighborhood Tabulation Areas; NYC Department of Health and Mental Hygiene, Heat Vulnerability Index.

Population layers: NYC Department of City Planning, Decennial Census data, native NTA2020 rows. Black means non-Hispanic; Hispanic includes any race. These use the same source and definitions as the bivariate maps, but different geographic units. Council boundaries: NYC Open Data, City Council Districts, pinned August 21, 2026; district numbers only, not member names or district demographic estimates.

The Heat Vulnerability Index is shown in its published neighborhood geography. It combines surface temperature, green space, home air conditioning and income. No AC is 100 minus the DOHMH households-with-AC percentage; it does not measure whether AC works or is used. Census shares are counts divided by total neighborhood population, rounded to one decimal place. Gray means missing data (including demographic shares in zero-population areas), never zero. Percentage color breaks are display intervals, not risk thresholds or the bivariate terciles.

II. No working air conditioning in use

Source: NYC Department of Health and Mental Hygiene, 2026 Heat-Related Mortality Report, Table 2, “Air conditioning presence among heat-stress decedents exposed to heat in homes,” 2016–2025. Download the table data.

The 25 people are those exposed to heat at home whose air-conditioning status was known. Thirteen had no AC; 12 had AC that was not working or not in use. None had a working AC in use. Exposure at home does not necessarily mean death occurred at home.

III. Number of days at or above 90°F

Source: New York City Panel on Climate Change projections, published by the Mayor’s Office of Climate and Environmental Justice in New York City Climate Projections: Extreme Events and Sea Level Rise. Data downloaded August 21, 2026.

Values are days per year with maximum air temperature at or above 90°F. The observed baseline covers 1981–2010; projections cover the 2030s, 2050s and 2080s. Lines show the 25th and 75th model percentiles, with shading for the 25th–75th and 10th–90th ranges. These are model ranges, not confidence intervals; no median is supplied by the source.

IV. Where heat vulnerability overlaps

Sources: NYC Department of Social Services, cooling-assistance application approvals and denials by ZIP code for the 2025 season, obtained by The Margin’s reporters; NYC Department of Health and Mental Hygiene, Heat Vulnerability Index; NYC Department of City Planning, Decennial Census data; and NYC Open Data, Modified ZIP Code Tabulation Areas and 2020 Census Tracts. Analysis by The Margin.

Applications are shown per 1,000 residents using the map’s population estimates. Neighborhood HVI scores are population-weighted to modified ZIP areas using 2020 Census tracts. Racial and ethnic shares use 2020 Census counts; Black means non-Hispanic, and Hispanic includes people of any race. Colors group each variable using tercile cutoffs. These maps show co-location, not causation; race is not an HVI input.

Citywide population shares use NYC Census totals; the citywide HVI average is calculated from scored neighborhoods, weighted by population. The application benchmark covers only mapped areas with application records. Missing application data are not treated as zero.
